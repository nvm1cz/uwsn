ABSTRACT Underwater wireless sensor networks (UWSNs) are commonly used for data transmission
underwater, detecting volcanic movements, and detecting water pollution. A UWSN is made up of acoustic
sensors powered by batteries. Replacement of these batteries is complicated because of the complex
underwater conditions of UWSNs. One strategy for fixing this issue is to extend the lifetime of UWSNs
by decreasing the energy they spend and enhancing their energy usage. This research proposes a set of
rules based on Energy-balanced unequal layering clustering (EULC) and PSO (Particle swarm optimization)
that enhances the performance of the lifetime of UWSNs. The proposed hybrid PSO-EULC combination
produces UWSNs with different layering based entirely on node depth, with Cluster head (CH) nodes
chosen using the PSO cost function. The hybrid PSO-EULC combination successfully balances the energy
used in UWSN nodes, thus extending sensor node lifetime, packet transmission cost, and network lifetime.
The results are compared with Energy Efficient Unequal Multilevel Clustering (EEUMC), Energy-balanced
reliable and effective clustering (EBREC), and LEACH with parameters such as residual energy, dead nodes,
and total packets received at different initial power levels of 1J,0.5J for 6400 bits, and 4000 bits of data
transmission. As per case 4 the residual energy of 44.4% improved, dead nodes 15% less, and 50.25% of
received packets is achieved by comparing it with the EEUMC algorithm.

As per the pioneer algorithm, the Low Energy Adaptive
Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH) is a graded protocol in which
multiple nodes send data to CHs. The CHs consolidate and
reduce this data before transmitting it to the sink or Base
Station(BS). Every SN employs a stochastic process in each
round to determine if it will serve as a CH. LEACH operates
under the premise that every SN has a broadcast capable
of communicating directly with the BS or the closest CH.
However, continuously broadcasting full power can lead to
energy waste.
Over a set number of rounds, denoted as P , nodes
that have served as CHs can be re-elected as CHs again.
P represents the desired proportion of CHs in the network.
Consequently, each node that is not a CH has a 1/P chance
of being selected as a CH again. Each non-CH node chooses
the nearest CH and joins that cluster after every round.
The CH then establishes a schedule for every SN in the
cluster to send its data. Non-CH nodes link with the CH
using Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA), following
the schedule established by the CH. They accomplish this
by transmitting the CH at the least power necessary to
communicate, effectively conserving energy. Based on these
challenges and issues, one of the main objectives is to
reduce the dead nodes, increase the lifetime of SNs, and
optimize CH selection using the proposed hybridization of
the PSO-EULC algorithm. The hybridization is built on the
EULC algorithm, which elects CH in a timely manner, but
it does not elect CH based on energy levels, depth, and
unequal level arrangement of SNs. PSO is hybridized to fulfill
the drawbacks of EULC by optimizing the CH selection
process by its behaviour of optimizing the problem based
on cost function designed to elect CH in a better way so
that the network remains active for a long time and SNs
save their energy levels using the optimization of this hybrid
technique.

II. RELATED WORKS
A review of recent literature highlights the vital role
of acoustic sensors in UWSNs for collecting data from
deep-sea environments [2]. Extensive research has led
to the development of various routing protocols aimed
at improving data transmission efficiency and extending
network lifetime [3]. Among them, the LEACH algorithm
enhances network performance through randomized rota-
tion and uniform clustering [4]. The foundational LEACH
protocol introduced adaptive clustering to improve energy
efficiency in sensor networks. The principles of dynamic
CH rotation and data aggregation is useful for optimizing
energy usage and increasing lifetime through intelligent
clustering [5], while EEHC adopts a hierarchical clustering
strategy that prolongs network activity. However, EEHC
may burden CHs, causing faster energy depletion [6].
To address this, a distributed CH selection approach was
introduced to balance energy usage, outperforming LEACH
in terms of longevity [7]. Bio-inspired techniques like
Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) have gained popularity
due to their ease of implementation, faster convergence,
and higher solution quality [8]. PSO has been effectively
used for optimal CH election by considering neighbor-
hood density and cluster compactness [9]. Complementing
this, the Enhanced Unequal Layered Clustering (EULC)
model introduces irregular layering based on node depth
to counter hotspot issues by forming variable-sized clusters
within layers [10]. However, methods such as DEBCR that
select CHs based on depth and non-residual energy can
result in inconsistent cluster structures in a 3D underwater
environment [11]. By integrating the strengths of both
PSO and EULC, a hybrid framework was developed to
reduce energy consumption, improve data transmission, and
minimize node death across operational rounds. This model
emphasizes residual energy and power levels of sensor
nodes over mere distance metrics [12]. A fitness function
is employed to optimize CH selection and enhance sensor
node longevity, addressing limitations observed in earlier
models that focused less on lifetime enhancement [13].
Techniques such as ridge-based routing from CHs to the
sink have also been introduced to reduce overall energy
drain [14]. From an application-level perspective, some
works analyze data loss and transmission errors through CHs.
In parallel, the potential of millimeter-wave communication
for deep underwater transmission at high frequencies has
been explored [15]. Although time-based scheduling is used
to prevent early node deactivation, irregular node placement
across varying depths necessitates CH election strategies
that consider both energy levels and spatial distribution—
factors often overlooked in traditional models [16]. To further
improve energy optimization, researchers have turned to
advanced metaheuristics. For example, Fei et al. utilized
moth flame optimization to enhance energy efficiency,
though challenges remain regarding CH election across layers
and sensor lifetime [17]. Other works adopt dynamic CH
rotation by setting energy thresholds, thus avoiding premature

in prolonging network life, they often depend on centralized

offer a more scalable and adaptive alternative. Hybrid

clustering-based optimization complements it by optimizing

metric introduces an effortless evaluation of path quality
by considering both link and node forwarding reliability,
where routing decisions are influenced by energy and
reliability metrics to optimize lifetime and robustness [26].
Node-level optimization has also been explored for target
tracking. Lv et al. proposed a dynamic selection algorithm
based on posterior Cramér–Rao lower bounds, integrating
node mobility and energy constraints for more accurate
and energy-efficient tracking [27]. This aligns well with
broader clustering efforts aimed at lifetime extension.
Several alternative hybrid metaheuristics have emerged. For
instance, Byeon et al. proposed a Hybrid Path Finder–V ortex
Search (HPF-VS) algorithm combined with Improved Dwarf
Mongoose Optimization (IDMO) to address issues like void
regions and depth variability [28]. Similarly, Wang et al.
introduced a fuzzy clustering model that dynamically selects
CHs based on residual energy, water depth, and sink
distance, emphasizing adaptive clustering in underwater
environments [29]. Other noteworthy contributions include
the GJERO-based HOSEMU model, which addresses energy
hole issues through sink mobility and hybrid optimiza-
tion [30], and the HGS-MPA method, which improves
routing through intelligent, energy-aware CH selection [31].
Protocols like EHDDAHRP introduce mechanisms such
as dynamic depth adjustment and semantic self-healing,
enhancing energy efficiency and fault tolerance in under-
water networks [32]. Metaheuristic approaches such as
GSA-based clustering, Red Piranha Optimization (RPO),
and hybrid fuzzy logic methods have shown promise in
optimizing CH selection and ensuring energy-aware data
transmission [33], [34], [35]. Similarly, dual-metaheuristic
models like BWF-SWO, and hybrid protocols like ESP
(which combines Cuckoo Search and Genetic Algorithms),
support efficient clustering and energy scheduling [36], [37].
Lastly, QHRP—a Q-learning-based hierarchical routing
protocol—illustrates the value of learning-based, adaptive
strategies in routing, which aligns well with the objectives
of clustering-based optimization for long-term underwater
network performance [38].

III. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND MOTIVATION
A set of strategies based on power-unequal layered clustering
has been developed to lower energy spending between cluster
communications. The EULC approach designs clusters of
different sizes within the same layer. The communication
radius is utilized to segment the depth of the network
distribution into distinct layers [39].
SNs assign weights by considering residual energy,
proximity to the sink, and the node’s position. The CH is
identified as the node with maximum weight. Each node
records the statistics of its adjacent CHs and calculates
averaging functions to all neighbouring CHs, taking into
account both the distances between the nodes and the residual
energy levels of the nodes. From the collected data, every
node determines the next CH.
The proposed protocol’s operation is based on the moti-
vation of a consolidated set of rules executed at the base
station, with an elevated node transmitting the data. The
recommended protocol is structured into rounds, with every
round beginning with an initialization part where clusters are
modelled. This is seen by a steady UWSN phase, in which we
utilized the same method. All nodes transmit data to the Sink,
indicating their remaining energy and distance at the start of
each setup phase. The Sink estimates the CH election stage
of all nodes using this information. To ensure that only a few
nodes with adequate power are chosen as CHs, nodes with

IV. CONTRIBUTION
The objective is to optimize CH selection, reduce the
energy consumption of SNs, improve packet delivery ratio,
reduce dead nodes ratio, and improve the lifetime of SNs
using the hybrid model of the EULC algorithm with PSO.
The contribution towards the goal is developing the EULC
algorithm and mapping its behaviour with the PSO algorithm
to find its effective CH selection process, which is the
primary task towards the objective, followed by comparing
it with the results of non-hybrid mode EEUMC, EBREC,
EULC, and LEACH. Simulations were conducted to evaluate
residual energy, the onset of dead nodes, and the number of
packets transmitted versus rounds, using initial energy levels
of 1 joule with a packet size of 6400 bits, and 0.5 joule
with a packet size of 4000 bits. The results demonstrate that
the PSO-EULC algorithm outperforms the other algorithms
considered for validation.
The specialty of the PSO-EULC hybridization is the even
distribution of energy across uneven layers, considering
the SNs closer to sink and farther in forming CHs. The
dynamical adjustments in the size of clusters based on
distance and energy from the sink are considered to reduce
energy consumption and prolong the lifetime of SNs. The
SNs with higher residual energy get elected as CHs on
an adaptive basis, ensuring equal energy levels across the
network. PSO is used to optimize the selection of CHs by
minimizing energy consumption and maximizing network
coverage, which ensures that the most energy-efficient SNs
are chosen as CHs. PSO explores the entire search space to
find the best set of CHs, avoiding local optima that traditional
algorithms might fall within desired levels. PSO adapts to
changes in the network with node deaths and energy depletion
by reevaluating the fitness function in every round. The
hybridization of PSO-EULC ensures energy efficiency, load
balancing, and extended lifetime of SNs. This traditional
approach outperforms all the other clustering algorithms.

In a three-dimensional UWSN scenario as shown in Figure 1,
there are three types of SNs: static SNs anchored to the
seabed, dynamic SNs float inside water, and sink floats on
surface. During the deployment phase of UWSN, SNs are
unevenly placed within a confined area, while the sink is
positioned on the surface. The subsequent assumptions apply
to this setup:
• All SNs are positioned within a confined volume,
sharing similar systems and energy characteristics
but possessing individual IDs; every node knows its
position.
• Every node may perform the functions of both normal
and CH nodes. All nodes can aggregate, record infor-
mation, and change transmitting strength based on the
distance between records.
• The sink is positioned on the top surface in a confined
space and may connect to all nodes in the network.
• The SNs gather statistics on a regular schedule. Nodes
within a layer send data to that layer’s CH, which
then transmits information to the CH one layer higher,
concluding with the CH at the top layer.

VI. PROPOSED HYBRID PSO-EULC ALGORITHM
The EULC algorithm is designed using an unequal layering
approach. It calculates the energy of nodes based on their
distance from the sink and the CH. CHs are rotated at
regular intervals following this strategy, ensuring that the
energy levels of all SNs remain balanced. This helps to
extend the overall lifetime of the network. The bio-inspired
optimization method known as Particle Swarm Optimiza-
tion (PSO) is based on the social behavior of swarms,
including fish schools or flocks of birds. PSO involves a
population of particles (possible solutions) that update their
locations and velocities to explore the solution space. The
swarm’s global best position and each particle’s best-known
position (personal best) are used to modify their trajectory.
The algorithm iteratively improves solutions by balancing
exploration (looking for new locations) and exploitation
(concentrating on potential places). PSO is frequently used
for issues including clustering, optimization, and machine
learning since it is computationally efficient and simple to
apply. It performs exceptionally well at managing intricate,
multi-dimensional areas. The PSO algorithm, as shown
in Algorithm 2, shows the optimization of the CH selection
process.

behaviour of SNs.Initial cluster formation is done using the
EULC algorithm, and CH selection is optimized based on
fitness functions such as residual energy, sink position, and
SN position. This is done using PSO, and the primary goal of
topology control is to identify k-CHs from the members of the
clusters to minimize the objective function, which is regarded
as an optimization problem. V arious conventional algorithms

A. RESIDUAL ENERGY
Residual energy is the power that remains in a sensor
node after several communication rounds. It contributes to
determining whether a node can continue to engage in

The proposed PSO-EULC algorithm integrates PSO for CH

computational complexity is derived as follows:

Let:
• N : Total number of sensor nodes
• P: Number of particles (population size)

• I : Number of PSO iterations
• K : Number of cluster heads (assumed fixed for each

Each particle evaluates a fitness function that depends on
the residual energy and distance of all nodes to the selected
CHs. Hence, each fitness evaluation costs O(N ). Over all
iterations and particles, the total PSO cost becomes:
OPSO = O(P × I × N )
Substituting:
OPSO = O(30 × 50 × 100) = O(150,000)
2. EULC COMPONENT
The EULC algorithm includes:
• Layering of all N nodes: O(N )
• Cluster formation and cost computation: O(N × K )
K = 3, the EULC complexity is:
OEULC = O(N + N × K ) = O(N × K ) = O(300)
3. TOTAL COMPLEXITY
The overall complexity is:
OTotal = OPSO + OEULC = O(P × I × N ) + O(N × K )
= O(150,000 + 300) ≈ O(150,000)
Although PSO-EULC exhibits higher computational com-
plexity compared to conventional methods such as LEACH,
EEUMC, and EBREC, its performance gains justify the
additional cost:
• Energy Efficiency: PSO ensures optimal CH selection
by minimizing intra-cluster distance and maximizing
residual energy, leading to reduced energy consumption
per round.
• Network Lifetime: Due to adaptive and depth-aware
clustering, PSO-EULC significantly improves node
survival and extends the FND and LND.
• Stability and Balance: Unlike LEACH and EBREC,
which rely on random or fixed heuristics, PSO-EULC
maintains better energy balance and uniform coverage.
• Scalability: Despite the O(P × I × N ) complexity, the
PSO module can be parallelized, and the cost remains
acceptable for moderate-sized UWSNs.
Hence, PSO-EULC achieves a superior trade-off between
computational cost and performance, especially in critical

VIII. CONCLUSION
This research presents an optimized CH selection technique
based on the proposed hybrid PSO-EULC algorithm. The
EULC algorithm technique established a node clustering
system that primarily focuses on node depth and residual
energy. Additionally, using an improved PSO approach, clus-
ter formation considers criteria such as the distance between
cluster nodes and the CH, the range between the CH node
and the sink, and optimal energy consumption. The proposed
hybrid PSO-EULC method has demonstrated a significantly
lower node loss rate than algorithms like EEUMC, EBREC,
LEACH, and EULC, extending the network’s lifespan. The
average improvement percentage in four cases shows that
the PSO-EULC hybrid method results in a 15% lower
death rate of nodes compared to the EEUMC method,
despite having 44.4% less residual energy. Additionally,
the average packet reception rate is 50.25%. It can be
concluded that the proposed PSO-EULC hybrid algorithm
produces better results compared to individual methods.
In the future, various hybridizations of PSO methods and
new bio-inspired algorithms will be analyzed to optimize
CH selection, and performance analysis will be conducted to
evaluate their effectiveness. In addition, we plan to validate
the proposed method using real underwater sensor datasets
or field deployment data, which would further strengthen the
practical applicability of our approach. The research work
illustrates that the proposed scheme significantly reduces the
power consumption of SNs compared to traditional methods.
Additionally, future works will focus on independently
optimizing various network parameters.
